Manufacturing data science · Portfolio case study

Weight prediction under process shift

Can injection-molding telemetry estimate a completed part’s weight—and remain reliable when process conditions change?

Steven Bangdiwala · Independent portfolio study · Python, scikit-learn, LightGBM, Polars, Streamlit

These are separately fitted retrospective benchmarks. Their gap describes performance under two evaluation designs; it is not a paired causal estimate of process shift. No authoritative product tolerance is available.

01 · Contribution

What I built, and why the details matter

I implemented the source adapter, source audit, evaluation memberships, model and signal comparisons, uncertainty checks, predictive explanations, and the local dashboard and static report. Bogedale et al. collected and released the source measurements; the modeling libraries supply the estimators.

Source-safe preparation

The adapter verifies pinned source bytes and joins each machine cycle to its measured part. It preserves native timestamps, nulls and field lineage. Ninety-two released signal-only cycles have no supported weight target, so they remain outside the prediction study.

Leakage-safe comparisons

Fixed row memberships separate fitting, tuning, calibration and evaluation. Scaling and signal compression learn only from permitted training rows. Weight and experimental context do not enter the predictors.

A bounded complexity test

Mean, Ridge and partial least squares (PLS) establish scalar baselines. Engineered summaries and compressed pressure/flow traces test signal shape. LightGBM tests a nonlinear model family without expanding the search after evaluation.

A decision gate that can fail

Prediction intervals test uncertainty. A development-only distance screen checks whether unfamiliar inputs can rank prediction errors well enough to study selective measurement. The screen failed; that next study was skipped.

02 · Prediction results

Low error within known conditions did not transfer

The scalar PLS baseline reached 0.114 g pooled MAE for represented conditions and 0.523 g across the omitted experiments. Its predefined scientific summary gives each experiment equal weight: 0.503 g MAE. The pooled value instead gives every evaluated cycle equal weight.

The error was systematic for experiment 23. PLS underpredicted all 303 held-out parts by an average of 0.798 g. That directional bias is evidence of a model failure in this population; it does not identify a physical cause.

Did detailed signals or nonlinear models help?

Some pressure/flow representations improved the aggregate error, but every augmented representation was worse than scalars on at least one omitted experiment. LightGBM’s summary features gave a small aggregate improvement; compression did not improve its aggregate result. Scalar PLS had the lowest equal-experiment MAE among the predefined comparisons. No deployment model was selected from those outer scores.

03 · Uncertainty results

Familiar-condition calibration missed the shifted errors

A separate uncertainty branch chooses scalar PLS or LightGBM on development data, then uses reserved calibration cycles to estimate a split-conformal error margin. The margin defines an interval around each prediction. For whole-experiment evaluation, neither fitting nor calibration sees the omitted experiment.

Calibration learns an error margin from familiar conditions. It does not automatically enlarge that margin when a new condition causes larger errors. Split-conformal coverage relies on statistically interchangeable calibration and future examples—the exchangeability assumption. These process changes do not establish that assumption.

Uncertainty method, selection boundary and full results

Could input novelty identify more reliable predictions?

The study tested one fixed distance score: the average distance to five nearby training cycles after rescaling the scalar features using training data. Keeping the closest 75% of development predictions had to reduce MAE by at least 10% in every held-out-experiment setup.

Model selection reused the development labels, so the screen is selection-affected and may be optimistic. No measurement savings or safe measurement-skipping policy was demonstrated.

What predictive explanations can tell us

Permutation importance asks how much prediction error changes when a feature’s values are shuffled. The leading feature changed across evaluated populations, and correlated temperature channels often acted as condition proxies. Marginal training-range departures describe unfamiliar inputs. Neither diagnostic establishes a universal sensor ranking, a causal mechanism, or a validated drift detector.

Decision implication: retain physical measurement. A proposed follow-up would collect labeled examples across a broader operating envelope—the combinations and ranges of supported process conditions—and evaluate complete conditions, lots or machines prospectively.

04 · Methods and data

Hold out conditions, not just individual cycles

The study uses 829 labeled cycles across 3 controlled experiments from scatimdata Dataset 2. A stacking-box part was molded from glass-fiber-reinforced nylon 6 (PA6-GF30). Each cycle provides 16 scalar machine measurements and pressure/flow traces with 2,048 samples per channel. Physical weight in grams is the reference outcome.

A scalar is one number, such as peak pressure or injection time. A trajectory is the sequence recorded during the cycle. Prediction occurs after the machine cycle is complete; this is a study of virtual measurement, not early-cycle control.

1. Join & auditMatch cycle identity to weight; retain source meanings.
2. Reserve rowsWithhold a whole experiment; keep calibration separate.
3. Fit & compareTune on development experiments; transform within folds.
4. EvaluateMeasure error and coverage on the reserved population.
  • Represented conditions: training contains cycles from all three experiment groups; different cycles are reserved for evaluation. This mainly tests interpolation within familiar conditions.
  • Unseen experiment: all cycles from one experiment are excluded from fitting and calibration. The other two experiments supply development data. Repeat for experiments 15, 20 and 23.

70.45% of observed weight variation lay between the three groups. Their separation can make pooled scores look strong when all groups are represented. It motivates the harder whole-experiment benchmark.

Source and evaluation limitations

The initial audit inspected all three experiments before the later evaluation scope was fixed. The grouped benchmark is retrospective cross-validation, not an untouched prospective test. Adjacent cycles and three specific experiments do not support a universal factory claim.

Pressure and flow amplitudes remain in unresolved source-native units; elapsed time is in seconds. Moisture settings for experiment 15 disagree with the paper in two places. Mold-temperature units are inferred as °C. Geometry fields are retained but deferred because their scale and mapping are unresolved. There is no authoritative wall-clock chronology or part-weight specification.

The source paper studied random cross-validation within the available mixture of conditions. This implementation asks a different transfer question and uses different features and models. It is an extension, not an exact replication or a contradiction of the paper.
